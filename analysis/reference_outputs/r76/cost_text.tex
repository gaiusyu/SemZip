Across the twelve smaller files, \tool's geometric-mean encoding and decoding throughputs are 19.5\% and 47.1\% of DeLog's, and it is slower than DeLog on every file in both directions. Weighted by bytes, \tool\ encodes at 2.71 and decodes at 16.95~MB/s versus 21.09 and 45.80~MB/s for DeLog.
On twenty sampled blocks of each of the four larger files, which hold 98\% of all bytes, \tool\ encodes at 8.53 and decodes at 46.26~MB/s versus 28.56 and 81.78~MB/s for DeLog (29.9\% and 56.6\%, byte-weighted). The Large column of Table~\ref{tab:external-speed} uses only HDFS and Thunderbird, where every method restores its blocks, and there LogShrink+R and LogReducer+R encode at 0.76 and 0.91~MB/s.
Construction is a one-time offline cost per log source. Byte-weighted, \tool\ encodes 3.3$\times$ (four larger files) to 7.8$\times$ (twelve smaller files) more slowly than DeLog. Where \tool\ is smaller, it suits cold archives better than hot or near-line logs.
