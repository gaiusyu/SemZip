Across the twelve smaller files, \tool's geometric-mean encoding and decoding throughputs are 19.5\% and 47.1\% of DeLog's, and it is slower than DeLog on every file in both directions. Geometric means favor small files, where process start-up dominates both tools. Weighted by bytes, \tool\ encodes at 2.71 and decodes at 16.95~MB/s versus 21.09 and 45.80~MB/s for DeLog.
On twenty sampled blocks of each of the four larger files, which hold 98\% of all bytes, \tool\ encodes at 8.53 and decodes at 46.26~MB/s versus 28.56 and 81.78~MB/s for DeLog (29.9\% and 56.6\%, byte-weighted). The Large column of Table~\ref{tab:external-speed} uses only HDFS and Thunderbird, where every method restores its blocks. There, LogShrink+R and LogReducer+R encode at 0.76 and 0.91~MB/s.
Construction is a one-time offline cost per log source. Five syntheses, the gate, and selection amortize over every later block, and archival storage is written once and restored rarely. For sources where it is smaller, \tool\ suits cold archives that tolerate an encoder 3.3$\times$ (four larger files) to 7.8$\times$ (twelve smaller files) slower than DeLog's, byte-weighted. For hot or near-line logs, DeLog's faster encoding is the better trade.
