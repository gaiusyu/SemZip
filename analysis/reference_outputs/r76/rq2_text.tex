Table~\ref{tab:attribution} separates the contributions. With the same pipeline, backend, storage fitting, and runtime but no program, the empty program averages 72.59$\times$ (geometric mean 48.02$\times$) and is smaller than DeLog on only 3/16 files. The programs of the deployed plans reduce summed archive bytes by 6.9\% and account for 12 of \tool's 15 wins over DeLog. One greedy synthesis (SemZip-1) reaches 88.84$\times$ but is larger than the empty program on HealthApp and HPC. The quality gate removes such rules (93.08$\times$, 14/16 files smaller than DeLog), and selection across five syntheses adds the rest (99.41$\times$, 15/16).
A frozen, hand-written library of common renderers (timestamps, IPv4, sizes, percentages, hexadecimal and decimal numbers) through the same gate and runtime does not reproduce this: it is larger than the empty program on 12/16 files. The gate removes one rule group at a time and never compares against the empty program. With a post-hoc empty-program floor decided on block 0, which leaves \tool\ unchanged, the floored library improves on the empty program on 4 files (Apache, Hadoop, Linux and Windows) but keeps the larger library plan on BGL, and the equally single-candidate Gated plan is smaller than it on 15/16 files. Official DeLog without its per-dataset regular expressions (DeLog-gen) is smaller than \tool\ on 0/16 files.
The matched replay (last column) stores exactly the same matched spans either literally or as latent values, so it separates representation from field separation. Latent storage saves 0.4--15.1\% on 12 of the 13 informative files and 3.10\% summed over the 12 fully replayed files. On HDFS, HPC and Thunderbird literal and latent storage give byte-identical archives, and on Windows' sampled blocks the latent form is 0.71\% larger. Over the 12 fully replayed files, literal storage of the separated spans is 23.5\% smaller than the empty program and latent storage a further 3.1\%: field separation carries most of the gain, and latent values add a smaller, file-dependent share.
