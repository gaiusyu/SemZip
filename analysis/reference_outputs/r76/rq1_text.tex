On complete files, \tool\ has the largest ratio on 15/16 files (Table~\ref{tab:external-ratios}). Against DeLog, the strongest baseline, it is smaller on 15/16 files and on 11/12 held-out suffixes, which no selection or fitting step reads. Its geometric-mean ratio is 65.17$\times$ versus 56.67$\times$ (63.07$\times$ versus 56.67$\times$ on suffixes) and its arithmetic mean 99.41$\times$ versus 86.70$\times$. Summed archive bytes, however, are 0.42\% larger than DeLog's: Thunderbird holds 48.5\% of all raw bytes and is the one file where DeLog wins (64.86$\times$ versus 67.68$\times$; its frozen program is not even 1\% smaller than the empty program on 79\% of later blocks; RQ3).
DeLog's released implementation, run as its own benchmark script does, reproduces our DeLog numbers exactly on Linux and BGL, but its paper reports ratios 2.7\% lower to 13.3\% higher per file (mean 88.06$\times$); against those published ratios \tool\ has the higher ratio on 14/16 files (geometric mean +11.5\%) and its summed bytes are 2.1\% larger (supplement).
LogShrink and LogReducer as released do not restore most files byte-exactly (they drop carriage returns, spaces, and leading zeros), so their +R variants store a counted per-line correction. \tool\ is smaller than LogShrink+R on 14/14 and than LogReducer+R on 15/15 reconstructed files; LogShrink could not encode or decode every block of Spark and Windows, and LogReducer+R failed the SHA check on part of Spark. High-effort general-purpose settings remain far behind: XZ9e averages 46.62$\times$ and Zstd19 38.85$\times$; a block-0 Zstd dictionary and the Zstd ultra setting are in the supplement.
