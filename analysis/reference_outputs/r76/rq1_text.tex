On complete files, \tool\ has the largest ratio on 15/16 files (Table~\ref{tab:external-ratios}). Against DeLog, the strongest baseline, it is smaller on 15/16 files and on 11/12 held-out suffixes, which no selection or fitting step reads. Its geometric-mean ratio is 65.17$\times$ versus 56.67$\times$ (63.07$\times$ versus 56.67$\times$ on suffixes) and its arithmetic mean 99.41$\times$ versus 86.70$\times$. Summed archive bytes, however, are 0.42\% larger than DeLog's: Thunderbird holds 48.5\% of all raw bytes and is the one file where DeLog wins (64.86$\times$ versus 67.68$\times$; RQ3 shows that its frozen program rarely beats the empty program on later blocks).
LogShrink and LogReducer as released drop carriage returns, collapse spaces, strip leading zeros, and (LogShrink) truncate integers wider than 32 bits, so neither restores most files; their adapted +R variants store the counted per-line correction. \tool\ is smaller than LogShrink+R on 14/14 and than LogReducer+R on 13/13 reconstructed files; LogShrink could not encode or decode every block of Spark and Windows, and LogReducer+R failed the SHA check on part of Spark. High-effort general-purpose settings remain far behind: XZ9e averages 46.62$\times$ and Zstd19 38.85$\times$; a block-0 Zstd dictionary and the Zstd ultra setting are in the supplement.
