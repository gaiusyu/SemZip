We first ask how \tool\ compares with existing compressors. On complete files (Table~\ref{tab:external-ratios}), it has the largest ratio on 15/16 files. Against DeLog, the strongest baseline by geometric mean, it is smaller on 15/16 files and on 11/12 held-out suffixes, which no selection or fitting step reads. Its geometric-mean ratio is 65.17$\times$ versus 56.67$\times$ (63.07$\times$ versus 56.67$\times$ on suffixes), and its arithmetic mean is 99.41$\times$ versus 86.70$\times$. However, its summed bytes are 0.42\% larger than DeLog's because of Thunderbird. This file holds 48.5\% of all raw bytes and is the one file where DeLog wins (64.86$\times$ versus 67.68$\times$). The same file makes \tool's summed bytes 9.11\% larger than LogNexus+R's, whose Thunderbird archive is 18.2\% smaller.
DeLog's released implementation, run as its own benchmark script does, reproduces our DeLog numbers exactly on Linux and BGL, but its paper reports ratios 2.7\% lower to 13.3\% higher per file (mean 88.06$\times$). Against these published ratios, \tool\ has the higher ratio on 14/16 files (geometric mean +11.5\%) and 2.1\% larger summed bytes (supplement).
LogShrink and LogReducer as released do not restore most files byte-exactly because they drop carriage returns, spaces, and leading zeros. Their +R variants therefore store a counted per-line correction. \tool\ is smaller than LogShrink+R on 14/14 and than LogReducer+R on 15/15 reconstructed files. LogShrink could not encode or decode every block of Spark and Windows, and LogReducer+R failed the SHA check on part of Spark. LogNexus~\cite{lognexus} restores the whitespace-separated token sequence rather than the bytes. Run as released with its paper\textquoteright s per-dataset thresholds, it averages 88.17$\times$ on our inputs, and its paper reports 88.20$\times$. Every block passes its token check, but only BGL is restored byte-exactly. \tool\ is smaller than LogNexus+R on 15/16 files (not on Thunderbird) and smaller than even its uncorrected archives on 14/16 (not on HPC and Thunderbird). High-effort general-purpose settings remain far behind, with XZ9e averaging 46.62$\times$ and Zstd19 38.85$\times$. The supplement reports a block-0 Zstd dictionary and the Zstd ultra setting.
