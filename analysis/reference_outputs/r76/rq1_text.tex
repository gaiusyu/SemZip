On complete files, \tool\ has the largest ratio on 15/16 files (Table~\ref{tab:external-ratios}). Against DeLog, the strongest baseline by geometric mean, it is smaller on 15/16 files and on 11/12 held-out suffixes, which no selection or fitting step reads. Its geometric-mean ratio is 65.17$\times$ versus 56.67$\times$ (63.07$\times$ versus 56.67$\times$ on suffixes) and its arithmetic mean 99.41$\times$ versus 86.70$\times$. Summed archive bytes, however, are 0.42\% larger than DeLog's. Thunderbird holds 48.5\% of all raw bytes and is the one file where DeLog wins (64.86$\times$ versus 67.68$\times$). \tool\textquoteright s frozen program is not even 1\% smaller than the empty program on 79\% of later blocks (RQ3). For the same reason LogNexus+R, whose Thunderbird archive is 18.2\% smaller than \tool\textquoteright s, has 8.3\% fewer summed bytes than \tool, although \tool\ is smaller on every other file.
DeLog's released implementation, run as its own benchmark script does, reproduces our DeLog numbers exactly on Linux and BGL, but its paper reports ratios 2.7\% lower to 13.3\% higher per file (mean 88.06$\times$). Against those published ratios \tool\ has the higher ratio on 14/16 files (geometric mean +11.5\%) and its summed bytes are 2.1\% larger (supplement).
LogShrink and LogReducer as released do not restore most files byte-exactly (they drop carriage returns, spaces, and leading zeros), so their +R variants store a counted per-line correction. \tool\ is smaller than LogShrink+R on 14/14 and than LogReducer+R on 15/15 reconstructed files. LogShrink could not encode or decode every block of Spark and Windows, and LogReducer+R failed the SHA check on part of Spark. LogNexus~\cite{lognexus} restores the whitespace-separated token sequence rather than the bytes. Run as released with its paper\textquoteright s per-dataset thresholds, its archives average 88.17$\times$ on our inputs (its paper reports 88.20$\times$), every block passes its token check, and only BGL is restored byte-exactly. \tool\ is smaller than LogNexus+R on 15/16 files (not on Thunderbird) and smaller than even its uncorrected archives on 14/16 (not on HPC and Thunderbird). High-effort general-purpose settings remain far behind. XZ9e averages 46.62$\times$ and Zstd19 38.85$\times$. A block-0 Zstd dictionary and the Zstd ultra setting are in the supplement.
