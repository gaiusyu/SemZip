We first ask how \tool\ compares with existing compressors. On complete files (Table~\ref{tab:external-ratios}), it has the largest ratio on 15/16 files. Against DeLog, the strongest baseline by geometric mean, it is smaller on 15/16 files and on 11/12 held-out suffixes. Its geometric-mean ratio is 65.17$\times$ versus 56.67$\times$ (63.07$\times$ versus 56.67$\times$ on suffixes), and its arithmetic mean is 99.41$\times$ versus 86.70$\times$. Its summed bytes are 0.42\% larger than DeLog's because of Thunderbird, which holds 48.5\% of all raw bytes and is the one file where DeLog wins (64.86$\times$ versus 67.68$\times$). On Thunderbird, the LogNexus+R archive is 18.2\% smaller, which makes \tool's summed bytes 9.11\% larger than LogNexus+R's.
DeLog's paper reports per-file ratios 2.7\% lower to 13.3\% higher than our DeLog runs (mean 88.06$\times$). Against these published ratios, \tool\ has the higher ratio on 14/16 files (geometric mean +11.5\%) and 2.1\% larger summed bytes.
LogShrink and LogReducer drop carriage returns, spaces, and leading zeros on most files. \tool\ is smaller than LogShrink+R on 14/14 and than LogReducer+R on 15/15 reconstructed files. LogShrink could not encode or decode every block of Spark and Windows, and LogReducer+R failed the SHA check on part of Spark. With its paper\textquoteright s per-dataset thresholds, LogNexus averages 88.17$\times$ on our inputs (88.20$\times$ in its paper) but restores only BGL byte-exactly. \tool\ is smaller than LogNexus+R on 15/16 files (not on Thunderbird) and than its uncorrected archives on 14/16 (not on HPC and Thunderbird). Denum\textquoteright s released format cannot restore its input byte-exactly, so we report its compression-only ratios. Even so, its archives are larger than \tool\textquoteright s on 16/16 files (mean 80.73$\times$ versus 99.41$\times$). General-purpose compressors at high effort remain far behind, with XZ9e averaging 46.62$\times$ and Zstd19 38.85$\times$.
