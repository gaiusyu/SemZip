On sixteen LogHub files, each decoded byte-exactly from its archive alone, \tool\ is smaller than DeLog on 11 of 12 held-out suffixes (geometric-mean ratio 63.1$\times$ versus 56.7$\times$) and 15 of 16 complete files, and smaller than LogShrink+R, LogReducer+R, LogLite-BL and XZ9e (+R: released tools plus a counted correction) on every file they restore; summed bytes are 0.42\% larger than DeLog's because \tool\ loses on the largest file. On four web-server logs never used during development it is smaller than DeLog (which has no rules for them) on all four, with 9.9\% fewer summed bytes, although LogShrink+R and LogReducer+R are 0.5--0.6\% smaller on one. Without its programs the same pipeline reaches a geometric mean of 48.0$\times$ instead of 65.2$\times$; field separation provides most of this gain and latent values a smaller, file-dependent share. Two further complete runs keep all four narrow wins over DeLog. Encoding is 3.3--7.8$\times$ slower than DeLog's (byte-weighted, large and small files).
