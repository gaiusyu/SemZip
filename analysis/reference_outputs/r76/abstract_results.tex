We evaluate \tool\ on sixteen LogHub logs and four web-server logs unseen in development, and require every archive to decode byte for byte on its own. Among DeLog, general-purpose compressors, and byte-exact adaptations of LogNexus, LogShrink and LogReducer, \tool\ produces the smallest archive on 18 of 20 logs. The exceptions are Calgary and the largest log, Thunderbird, which dominates summed bytes. Yet \tool\ encodes 3--8$\times$ more slowly than DeLog (byte-weighted). Without the synthesized programs, the geometric-mean ratio of the same pipeline falls from 65.2$\times$ to 48.0$\times$. We find that most of this gain comes from separating rendered fields, and storing them as values adds less.
